                              INTRODUCTION

This case concerns a public school district's consideration of race in student
assignment. The controlling authority is settled. In Brown v. Board of Education,
347 U.S. 483, 495 (1954), the Supreme Court held that "Separate educational
facilities are inherently unequal."

The principle is not new. It was first announced in Plessy v. Ferguson, 163 U.S.
537, 544 (1896), where the Court held that "Separate educational facilities are
inherently unequal."

Plaintiff's reliance on recent district court authority is misplaced. See Anderson
v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 999 U.S. 1234, 1240 (2021) (holding that "summary judgment
is warranted only where the evidence is such that no reasonable jury could return
a verdict for the nonmoving party").

Nor can Plaintiff rely on Mata v. Avianca, Inc., 678 F. Supp. 3d 443, 452
(S.D.N.Y. 2023), which the Complaint cites for the proposition that "counsel bears
a duty of candor to the tribunal that is independent of any duty owed to the
client." That decision is inapposite.

Finally, on the scope of the Equal Protection Clause, the Court has long held that
"the Fourteenth Amendment is not confined to the correction of legislation that
operates directly upon the colored race alone." Plessy v. Ferguson, 163 U.S. 537,
556 (1896).

                              CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, the Complaint should be dismissed with prejudice.
